# Research basis

How much people-analytics and forecasting research StaySignal stands on, and where it stops.

**In one line:** StaySignal is a methods project on synthetic data, built on published turnover and forecasting research. It includes its own pre-registered simulation study. It is not empirical research on real employees.

Every number in this repository comes from a synthetic company, Larkspur Systems, generated by a seeded program. The full verified reference list is in [SPEC.md section 28](../SPEC.md).

## 1. Turnover research behind the features

Each forecasting feature takes its *direction* from published evidence. Its *size* is set by the synthetic generator, so nothing here claims a published effect magnitude.

| Feature | Evidence | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tenure and pay | Rubenstein, Eberly, Lee and Mitchell 2018, a meta-analysis of voluntary turnover (Personnel Psychology 71:23) | |
| Pay relative to peers | Card, Mas, Moretti and Saez 2012 (AER 102:2981) | That study measured satisfaction and job-search intentions after peer-pay disclosure, not exits |
| Manager changes | Ballinger, Lehman and Schoorman 2010 (OBHDP 113:25); Li, Hausknecht and Dragoni 2020 (Org Sci 31:458) | The effect is conditional on leader-member exchange |
| Coworker departures | Felps et al. 2009 (AMJ 52:545) | That study measured coworkers' job search and embeddedness, not departures |
| Reorganization | Trevor and Nyberg 2008 (AMJ 51:259) | |
| Time since promotion | None found | Labeled a hypothesis feature, checked only against the synthetic truth |

**Two deliberate choices:**

- **No performance ratings.** They are left out of the sources and the model, because of rater bias, the curvilinear relationship between performance and turnover (Trevor, Gerhart and Boudreau 1997, JAP 82:44), and the risk of steering decisions about individuals (decision 0015 in [SPEC.md](../SPEC.md)).
- **The strongest predictors are not observable.** In the turnover literature they are withdrawal cognitions and job search, which no HR system records. The generator models them as a hidden per-person trait, so the forecast cannot see them, just as it could not in reality.

**Calibrated to public rates.** The synthetic company's base rates are tuned into realistic ranges: voluntary 10% to 15% a year, at or below the Information industry's quit rate in BLS JOLTS Table 22. Exit-reason codes are known to be unreliable (Campion 1991, JAP 76:199), so results by departure type carry that caveat.

## 3. The pre-registered study

**Question:** how do late HR corrections, incomplete observation and shared team shocks affect the calibration of workforce departure forecasts, and which handling choices restore it?

**How it was run:**

- The hypotheses, metrics and decision rules were written in [research/PREREGISTRATION.md](../research/PREREGISTRATION.md) on 2026-10-12, before any run. Every result records the file's SHA-256 (`a32129ef9cde...`), and a test fails if the file changes after a result exists.
- Each condition was changed one at a time around a clean base world, over 20 seeds and 8 quarterly forecast origins, with a 3-month horizon.
- Equivalence was tested with two one-sided tests (TOST, alpha 0.05) on a seed bootstrap. The bounds were an observed-over-expected ratio (O/E) within 0.95 to 1.05, and 80% interval coverage within 75% to 85%.
- Run: `uv run --project engine staysignal research run --seeds 1-20` on 2026-10-13 ([results/research-2026-10-13.json](../results/research-2026-10-13.json)).

| Hypothesis | Verdict | What was measured |
|---|---|---|
| H1: counting only matured periods fixes late-reported terminations | counts against | Matured O/E was not shown equivalent to 1 at any late share: 0.958, 0.954, 0.951 |
| H2: treating a missing extract as censored fixes it | counts against | Censored O/E 0.965; treating it as no events gave 1.026 |
| H3a: shock-aware ranges keep their coverage when teams share shocks (company and organizations) | consistent | At the largest shock, 80% coverage was 77.5% shock-aware versus 74.3% assuming independence |
| H3b: the same, for teams | consistent | Shock-aware PIT distance 0.029, inside the 0.05 limit |
| H4: deduplicating departure records fixes duplicates | counts against | Deduplicated O/E 0.958; not deduplicated 0.917 |
| H5: both fixes together (exploratory) | descriptive | Matured and shock-aware: O/E 0.994, coverage 77.0%, both shown equivalent. Naive and independence: O/E 1.030, coverage 74.9%, neither shown equivalent |

**Why H1, H2 and H4 count against:** the clean base world already over-predicts slightly (O/E about 0.96). Each fix moves the forecast in the right direction but lands near the 0.95 bound, so equivalence can't be shown. The results are reported as found, and the pre-registration was not edited after the fact.

## 4. Measurement and simulated respondents

- **Validated scales.** The Scenario Lab's draft pilot items point to an established scale where one exists, and recommend using it instead: Colquitt 2001 for organizational justice (JAP 86:386), and Rizzo, House and Lirtzman 1970 for role conflict and ambiguity (ASQ 15:150).
- **Simulated personas are hypothesis generation, not data.** The Lab runs synthetic personas on CAMEL-AI's OASIS (Yang et al. 2024). It states the known limits of LLM-simulated respondents: Argyle et al. 2023, Bisbee et al. 2024, Santurkar et al. 2023, Dominguez-Olmedo et al. 2024, and Wang, Morgenstern and Dickerson 2025. Its output never feeds the forecast.

## 5. Governance and regulation

- **Action audit with adverse-impact screens:** the four-fifths rule plus Fisher's exact test, in the spirit of the Uniform Guidelines (29 CFR 1607).
- **[Model card](model-card.md):** its intended use excludes any statement or decision about an individual, and no protected attribute is a feature.
- **[Regulatory notes](regulatory-notes.md):** Title VII, the ADEA and the Uniform Guidelines; Illinois HB 3773; NYC Local Law 144; EU AI Act Annex III; UK GDPR. Not legal advice.
- **An [employee notice template](employee-notice-template.md).**

## 6. What this does not show

- That any effect holds for real HR data. Reporting delays, shocks and duplicates in real systems have unknown structure.
- Causal effects of any feature or intervention.
- Anything about individuals. Outputs are cohort-level only.
- That the model beats a simple baseline. At company level it does not beat the trailing 12-month rate on point accuracy. Its value is calibrated ranges, explanation and governance. See the Trust page and [results/backtest-2026-10-12.json](../results/backtest-2026-10-12.json).
