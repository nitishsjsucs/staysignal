# Pre-registration: what breaks the calibration of workforce departure forecasts, and what fixes it

Registered 2026-10-12, before any research run. Every results file of this study records the SHA-256 of this file, and a test fails if the file changes after a result exists. A change after the first result is logged under Deviations below with its date and reason; the original text stays.

Everything here runs on a synthetic company generated by a seeded program (Larkspur Systems, SPEC.md section 6). Results test methods on a world with a known truth; they are not evidence about any real workforce.

## Question

How do late HR corrections, incomplete observation and shared team shocks affect the calibration of workforce departure forecasts, and which handling choices restore it?

## Design

- Model: the landmark discrete-time competing-risks multinomial logit of SPEC.md section 9.1, with the nested Monte Carlo intervals of section 9.3 (parameters from the sandwich covariance, one fit per origin; no 200-refit bootstrap).
- Base world: the `research` profile (canonical structure) with team shocks at sigma 0.25 and organization shocks at sigma 0.125, contagion on, no planted data problems and no drift.
- Arms: one factor at a time around the base world, plus one interaction arm. Each arm crosses a data condition with a handling choice.
- Seeds: 20 per arm (seeds 1 to 20; the same seeds in every arm).
- Origins: 8 quarterly forecast origins per seed; horizon 3 months (voluntary departures).
- Actuals: always the generator's clean final truth, so a data problem in training can never be offset by the same problem in the actuals.
- Cohorts: the company and each organization (company-level hypotheses); teams for H3b only.

| Factor | Data condition (levels) | Handling compared |
|---|---|---|
| A. Late reporting of terminations | share recorded 30 to 75 days late: 0, 0.15, 0.30 | naive (training rows up to the origin; unreported exits look like stays) versus matured (a training row counts only when its period ended at least 75 days before the origin) |
| B. Missing extract | one monthly HRIS termination extract missing from training, or none | treated as no events versus treated as censored (the affected person-periods dropped) |
| C. Shared shocks, contagion off | sigma_team 0, 0.25, 0.5 with sigma_org half of it | independence intervals (process and parameter layers only) versus shock-aware intervals (all layers) |
| D. Duplicate departure records | share 0 or 0.05 | not deduplicated versus deduplicated |
| A x C | late share 0.30 with sigma_team 0.5 | matured and shock-aware versus naive and independence |

## Hypotheses, metrics and decision rules

Primary metric for H1, H2 and H4: the mean observed over expected (O/E) of voluntary departures across seeds and origins, company level. Primary metric for H3: the share of 80% intervals that contain the actual count.

Equivalence bounds: O/E within [0.95, 1.05]; coverage within 5 percentage points of nominal (75% to 85% for 80% intervals). Equivalence is tested with two one-sided tests (TOST) at alpha 0.05 on a seed bootstrap (2,000 resamples of the 20 seeds; origins kept together within a seed). Smallest effect of interest: an O/E shift of 0.05; a coverage drop of 5 points.

- **H1 (late reporting).** Naive O/E rises above 1 as the late share grows; matured O/E is equivalent to 1 at every share. Counts against H1: matured O/E outside the bounds at share 0.30, or naive O/E inside the bounds at share 0.30.
- **H2 (missing extract).** Treating the missing extract as no events gives O/E above 1; treating it as censored gives O/E equivalent to 1. Counts against H2: censored O/E outside the bounds, or no-events O/E inside them.
- **H3a (shared shocks, company and organizations).** O/E is equivalent to 1 at every sigma under both interval methods; independence coverage falls as sigma grows; shock-aware coverage is equivalent to nominal at every sigma. Counts against H3a: shock-aware coverage outside 75% to 85% at sigma 0.5, or independence coverage inside it at sigma 0.5.
- **H3b (shared shocks, teams).** Registered separately: the same statements for team-level cohorts, assessed with the randomized probability integral transform for counts (uniform under calibration; a Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance of 0.05 or less counts as calibrated). Counts against H3b: shock-aware PIT distance above 0.05 at sigma 0.5.
- **H4 (duplicates).** Without deduplication O/E falls below 1 (over-prediction, because duplicated exits inflate the training hazard); with deduplication O/E is equivalent to 1. Counts against H4: deduplicated O/E outside the bounds, or not-deduplicated O/E inside them at share 0.05.
- **H5 (interaction, exploratory).** Both handling choices are needed together: matured and shock-aware handling is calibrated where either alone is not. No decision rule; reported descriptively.

## Analysis plan

- For each arm, handling and level: mean O/E and 80% and 95% coverage across seeds and origins, the interval (Winkler) score, the seed-bootstrap 90% interval used by TOST, and the TOST p-value.
- Every number goes to `results/research-<date>.json` with the command, the date, the world generator version and the SHA-256 of this file, and to the Research page; bad results are reported as found.
- Features are computed without dbt by a fast path (`research/features_fast.py`) that runs the compiled staging and intermediate SQL and `pit.sql` on an in-memory DuckDB; the run checkpoints per seed so an interruption resumes.
- Before the full run, a 2-seed run is timed and the estimate recorded. CI runs 2 seeds by 2 origins on the small world.

## What this study cannot show

It cannot show that the same effects hold for real HR data, whose reporting delays, shocks and duplicates have unknown structure; that the model's features are the right ones; or anything about individuals. The generator's truth is known, which is the point: it lets each handling choice be judged against what actually happened.
